{\huge \bf Abstract}
\\
\\
\\
\\
\\
Physical models underpin process design, control, and digital twins, but building one for a process outside the libraries of modeling tools requires an engineer to collect the equations scattered across many documents and to assemble them into a model.
Our group aims to automate this work with an automated physical model builder (AutoPMoB).
This thesis addresses one of its steps: given a description of the desired model and its input and output variables, present the equations likely to serve in the model, drawn from an equation database built from multiple documents; the engineer then assembles and checks the model from these candidates.
Because an equation that contains a variable other than the inputs and outputs is useful only together with an equation that determines that variable, the value of a candidate depends on the equations presented with it, so the method judges each candidate by its relation to other equations, not by its relevance to the query alone.

We propose a two-stage retrieval method.
The first stage narrows the database by text similarity and variable overlap, and the second stage reranks the candidates with a multilayer perceptron that reads, beside seven query--equation features, three set-aware features that compare each candidate with a reference set of equations: complementarity, coherence, and domain agreement.
A learned greedy variant selects one equation at a time and compares each candidate with the equations already selected.
To evaluate the method, we built a database of 11,146 equations from 361 sources and 1,731 evaluation cases, 1,000 of which a program synthesized by giving each equation an output variable of its own; we split the sources so that no document leaks between training and testing, and we chose the hyperparameters of the methods, the values fixed before training, on validation cases.

% source: experiments/kato1020/main.json（Recall@20 A 0.906 → 0.981・B 0.825 → 0.966、MAP A 0.690 → 0.860・B 0.626 → 0.832、
%         wins 10）; mech.json; pfi.json; greedy.json（K>=8 train − static MAP +0.032）; case_outcomes_final.json
With a first stage that keeps every ground-truth equation among its 2,000 candidates, the method raises Recall@20 over classical retrieval from 0.906 to 0.981 in the mixed setting and from 0.825 to 0.966 in the synthesized-only setting, and mean average precision from 0.690 to 0.860 and from 0.626 to 0.832, on all 10 stratified splits.
Analyses attribute most of the accuracy to the features that read the variables equations share, the specificity of an equation to the query variables above all; the set-aware features mainly raise the correct equations within the top 20, and topical similarity adds little.
Selecting the equations one at a time with learned greedy selection raises mean average precision by a further 0.032 on the cases with eight or more ground-truth equations.
Every remaining miss arises in the second stage, where no present feature separates the missed equations from the incorrect ones ranked above them.
