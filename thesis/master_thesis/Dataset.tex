\section{Equation Database Construction}
\label{sec:db}

We built an equation database of 11,146 equations drawn from 361 sources. Of these sources, 329
are documents and contribute 10,476 equations. The remaining 32 sources are reference equation
sets that Gemini 2.5 Pro\cite{Gemini25} generated field by field to cover textbook fundamentals;
these 670 equations have no source document.
% source: unified_equations.json 実測（式 11,146、source_id 361 種。handbook_* 以外 329 種 = 10,476 式、
%         handbook_* 32 種 = 670 式。handbook_chemeng は build_equation_graph.py、他 31 種は expand_dataset.py、gemini-2.5-pro）

We collected the documents in three steps; \ref{app:papers} lists the search terms of
every field.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Early in this work, we collected 24 papers and textbooks on continuous stirred-tank
    reactors (CSTRs), biodiesel reactions, and process control by hand, and added 15 papers that a
    search on the scholarly search service Semantic Scholar retrieved automatically, 39 documents
    in all.
  \item To extend the database beyond chemical engineering, we chose one search term for each of
    32 fields, such as reaction engineering, heat transfer, electrochemistry, bioprocesses, and
    acoustics, and searched the scholarly database OpenAlex for ``\textit{search term}
    mathematical model differential equation.'' The search required open access to a PDF,
    publication in 2006 or later, and more than 10 citations, and it admitted articles, reviews,
    book chapters, preprints, reference entries, and reports. We took the 30 results most relevant
    to the search term, kept those whose title and abstract contain two or more words of the
    search term (words of four or more letters) and that have a PDF address, and tried to download
    the top 10 of them, replacing none that failed.
  \item For each field that still had fewer than 10 PDFs, we prepared three to five other search
    terms (ten for optics), took the 20 results most relevant to each, relaxed the citation
    condition to more than five and then to more than three citations (the latter also admitting
    publication from 2001 on; for optics alone, we finally dropped the citation condition),
    merged the results, ordered them by citation count, and downloaded PDFs until the field held
    10.
\end{enumerate}
The target of 10 documents per field counted the PDFs obtained, not whether equations could be
extracted from them. The searches yielded 306 PDFs, of which 290 contributed equations to the
database, 7 to 10 per field. Nobody screened the documents by hand, so some have little to do with
physical models --- a review of the theory of star formation, for instance, entered the field of
chemical engineering fundamentals --- and different searches retrieved 35 documents more than once,
adding 44 duplicate sources.
% source: docs/加藤先生コメント2_事実確認_2026-10-20.md 項目 1（fetch_papers_32domains.py: filter・関連度の順・30 件・
%         上位 10 件、fetch_papers_fallback{,2,3}.py・fetch_optics_only.py: 別の検索語・被引用数の順・10 個まで;
%         PDF 306 個、DB に式がある 290 出典、分野ごとに 7〜10 報、duplicate_works.csv）。日本語版報告 §3.1 と同じ

A large language model, Gemini, extracted the equations. Gemini 2.5 Pro extracted those of 320 of
the 329 documents; Gemini extracted the 9 earliest documents before we standardized on Gemini 2.5
Pro, and no record keeps the version used for them. The model read the PDF of a document and wrote
out its numbered equations, those of the appendices included, in \LaTeX{}, and for each equation
it returned, in a prescribed JSON format, the variable symbols with their definitions, a context
text that explains the physical meaning of the equation from the surrounding passage, and a domain
label (\ref{app:prompts} gives the prompt). The model kept the symbols as printed, and we
normalized none of them, except that a rule added the Laplace variable $s$ and time $t$ to the
variable sets of 277 equations.
% source: 抽出はすべて Gemini（本人確認 2026-10-21、親セッション経由）。初期の 9 報は 2026-03-16 より前の抽出で版の記録がない
%         （当時のコードの既定は gemini-2.5-flash）。extract_equations.py EXTRACTION_PROMPT（"all numbered equations and equations
%         found in the appendices"、記号は印刷どおり）; fix_dataset_quality.py（277 式に s と t を規則で追加）。
%         旧記載（2026-10-12、cc9301e）「追加した文献は Claude で抽出」は誤り。
The database grew from 3,244 to 11,146 equations during this work, and the added documents span
engineering fields beyond chemical engineering.

Two properties of the database govern the difficulty of retrieval, and we state them here because
every later result depends on them. First, the database holds 13,220 distinct variable symbols, and
the method matches variables by their symbols alone (Section~\ref{sec:formulation}). Of the 6,374
symbols that appear in two or more equations, 4,208 carry definitions that differ between
equations. Most differences only paraphrase one quantity (``time'' and ``time variable''), but some
symbols name different quantities: $k$ denotes a reaction rate constant, a thermal conductivity,
and a wavenumber in different equations. The method treats each such symbol as one variable.
% source: unified_equations.json 実測（eq_vars の和集合 13,220 種）; db_symbols_in_2plus_equations.csv
%         （2 本以上の式に現れる 6,374 種のうち定義が違う 4,208 種。事実確認 項目 9.1）
Second, the domain labels are free text written by the language models rather than entries of a
fixed taxonomy: the 11,146 equations carry 1,998 distinct labels, 27.9\% of which occur exactly
once, and a single source carries 6.36 labels on average. Exact agreement between two labels is
consequently rare; Section~\ref{sec:nullresult} tests whether merging the labels helps.
% source: unified_equations.json 実測（1,998 ラベル、singleton 27.9\%、source あたり 6.36 種）

\section{Test Cases}
\label{sec:cases}

Each test case pairs a query --- a description with its input and output variables --- with a
ground-truth equation set for the requested model. We formed the ground truths in three ways,
giving three families of cases (Table~\ref{tab:families}). Excluding 91 cases in which the inputs
leave some unknowns undetermined (see below), we evaluate on 1,732 cases. Every family takes its
input and output variables from the symbols of the ground-truth equations.
% source: training_cases.json variant_type 集計（original 92; multisource_* 731 → 91 件を除いて 640;
%         dae_X1..X10 各100 = 1,000）; experiments/kato1020/excluded_cases.json（91 件、setting_A_after 1,732）

A language model formed the ground truth of the single-source family (92 cases). We gave Gemini
2.5 Pro a catalog that listed, for each equation of the database at the time (3,244 equations),
only its identifier, which names its source, its domain label, and its variable symbols --- neither
the expression nor the context text. The catalog went to the model in 11 batches of at most 300
equations ordered by domain label, and each batch asked for 13 cases (\ref{app:prompts}
gives the prompt). The prompt asked for physically meaningful cases, each with a description,
input and output variables, and a ground-truth set built from the listed equations, and it
required that the inputs determine every output and that the set be sufficient and minimal, with
no redundant equation. Of the 123 cases the model returned, we kept the 92 whose ground-truth
equations all exist in the database, whose input and output variables all appear in the
ground-truth equations, and whose ground truth, once the inputs are given, has as many unknowns as
equations. The count of 92 therefore does not correspond to the number of sources. In 91 of them the ground
truth draws on one source, which gives the family its name; the remaining case combines equations
of two textbooks, Seborg et al.\cite{Seborg} and Marlin's textbook on process control, because the
batches followed the domain labels rather than the sources and the prompt did not require a single
source. The ground truth is the set that the model judged minimal, not a model that a source
presents, and we have not verified its minimality; 67 of the 92 cases need a single equation, and
61 draw only on the generated reference sets.

%         20 万字以上なら分野ラベルの辞書順に 300 式以下に分ける、1 回 max(10, 150/回数) 件、gemini-2.5-pro）;
%         filter_solvable_cases.py（123 件 → 92 件）; 2 出典の 1 件は core_019_v1（Seborg ほか 3 版と Marlin）;
%         training_cases.json 実測（K=1 が 67 件、正解式がすべて handbook_* の件が 61 件）。事実確認 項目 3

The cross-source family (640 cases) combines two or three equations from different sources. A
program built 496 of these cases (500 built, minus 4 excluded below) in five steps.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item It sorted the domain labels into ten categories --- reactor, heat transfer, mass transfer,
    fluid, thermodynamics, control, bioprocess, electrochemistry, combustion, and separation --- by
    a hand-written word list for each category: a label belongs to a category when it contains one
    of the words or is contained in one.
  \item A hand-written list fixed 15 pairs of categories that chemical engineering often combines,
    such as reactor with heat transfer and reactor with control; the program used 14 of them.
  \item Within these pairs of categories, the program enumerated pairs of equations from different
    sources that share at least two variable symbols. It compared the definitions of the shared
    symbols only against nine fixed pairs of words, such as ``temperature'' and ``time constant,''
    and dropped a pair of equations only when the two words of such a pair appeared in the two
    definitions. In 471 of the 496 cases the shared symbols therefore carry definitions that differ
    between the two equations: mostly paraphrases, but sometimes different quantities, as when the
    symbol $y$ denotes an output in one equation and a spatial coordinate in the other.
  \item It preferred pairs that contain a differential equation. From each selected pair it chose
    as outputs one variable that appears only in the first equation and one that appears only in
    the second, declared all other variables inputs, and built up to five cases from one pair with
    different choices of outputs.
  \item It wrote each description by inserting the shared symbols into an English template fixed
    for each pair of categories; no language model took part.
\end{enumerate}
Ten of these cases pair two sources that hold the same document, including what appears to be a
whole textbook and an excerpt of one of its chapters. The remaining 144 cases are 33 combinations that the language
model we used as a coding assistant (Claude) wrote directly into the program as physically
meaningful, and 111 variants of them (198 built, minus 87 excluded below) with reselected input and
output variables. No fixed prompt produced the 33 combinations, so we cannot show one; a check
confirmed that each of them, once the inputs are given, has as many unknowns as equations. A
variant enumerates every combination of as many variables of the ground-truth equations as the set
has equations, shuffles the combinations with random seed 42, and takes the first six that differ
from the original input and output variables; the chosen variables become the outputs and the rest
the inputs. The variants keep the description of their original case, so the descriptions of the
111 variants do not match their input and output variables. In 105 of the 144 cases (24 of the 33
and 81 of the 111), no two ground-truth equations share a variable symbol.

We also built 1,015 augmented cases from the single-source cases and from 25 cases of the
language model that failed the checks above: 738 reselect the input and output variables of a case
and keep its ground truth, 276 prefix its description with a fixed phrase (no language model took
part), and one swaps its input and output variables. The swap forms its own type because it follows
a different rule from the reselection; of the 123 swaps we built, only one kept as many unknowns as
equations. An augmented case nearly duplicates its original, so no experiment in this thesis uses
the augmented cases, for training or for evaluation.

The evaluation ground truths meet requirements 1--3 of Section~\ref{sec:formulation}, but these
requirements only count variables and equations, so we also checked whether each unknown can be
assigned to an equation of its own, one to one. In 91 cross-source cases the assignment fails: one
ground-truth equation contains input variables only, so some unknowns stay undetermined once the
inputs are given. Of these 91 cases, 87 are variants with reselected input and output variables and
4 are program-built. We exclude them from both training and evaluation, which leaves the 1,732
cases. Requirement~3 holds trivially in 1,725 of them, which contain no variable other than the
input and output variables, and requirement~4 cannot be checked, because the database does not mark
which variables are constants.

\section{Synthesized Case Generation}
\label{sec:synth}

Our earlier cases were biased toward small and purely algebraic equation sets, so we generated
cases with more equations, mixing differential and algebraic equations, by a program. The
procedure, proposed by Mr.~Kato, fixes the target size $X$, the number of equations the ground
truth should hold, and works in two phases. First, it picks a differential equation of the
database at random and takes one of its differentiated variables as an output. It then repeatedly
adds an equation that contains a variable, other than that output, of the equations collected so
far, choosing at random among the equations of the first equation's source when any qualifies and
among those of other sources otherwise, until the set holds $X$ equations. Second, it goes through
the added equations from the second on, chooses at random one variable of each that is not yet an
output, and adds it to the outputs; every variable left over becomes an input. The resulting sets
have as many output variables as equations and, once the inputs are given, as many unknowns as
equations; each equation contributes an output of its own, so every unknown can be matched to its
own equation.

%         先に X 本、その後 309-319 行で追加した式ごとに出力を 1 個、321-327 行で入力）。事実確認 項目 7.1。
%         旧記載「式を 1 本加えるたびに出力を 1 個選ぶ」はプログラムの順と違った（加藤先生の注釈 R18）;
%         実測（2026-09-29）: 合成 1,000 件すべてで式と未知変数の完全マッチングが存在
We generated 100 cases for each $X = 1, \dots, 10$, giving 1,000 cases, 959 of which draw on a
single source. A large language model, Claude Sonnet 4.5\cite{ClaudeSonnet45}, writes only the
natural-language description (\ref{app:prompts} gives the prompt); the equation set, the
input variables, and the output variables all follow from the program.
% source: dae_generation_report.json "model"（claude-sonnet-4-5-20250929）; training_cases.json 実測（959 件が 1 出典）

A continuous stirred-tank reactor gives the smallest interesting example: once the inputs are
given, the mass-balance ordinary differential equation, the reaction-rate equation $r_A = k C_A$,
and the Arrhenius equation $k = A \exp(-E / RT)$ determine the three unknowns $C_A$, $r_A$, and $k$,
one equation each. \ref{app:example} shows one generated case in full. Because every
synthesized ground truth meets requirements 1--3 and admits this one-to-one assignment, a failure
to retrieve it is a failure of the method rather than of the dataset.

\section{Stratified Source-disjoint Splitting}
\label{sec:split}

We split the cases into training, validation, and test cases by source. Each case belongs to the
source that contributes most of its ground-truth equations. We shuffle the list of sources and
assign 20\% of them to testing, 20\% to validation, and 60\% to training. If cases whose ground
truths contain equations of the same source fell into both training and testing, the model would
see the test answers during training; assigning whole sources prevents this. The ratio fixes the
number of sources, not of cases, and sources hold different numbers of cases, so the numbers of
cases vary from split to split: 298 to 527 test cases and 216 to 349 validation cases in
Setting~A. The validation cases choose the constants of the methods (Section~\ref{sec:compared}).
% source: set_aware_reranker.py stratified_src_split; experiments/kato1020/baselines.json（設定 A の n_test 298〜527、
%         n_val 216〜349）。日本語版報告 §3.3 と同じ
Source-disjoint assignment, however, introduces a new problem: a random assignment of sources can
put the cases with many equations on one side and make the score depend on the draw rather than on
the method.

We therefore stratify the assignment. Among 400 random assignments of the sources we select the one
that minimizes the summed $L_1$ distance between the training and test frequency distributions, as
proportions, of four quantities per case: the number of ground-truth equations, the number of input
variables, the number of output variables, and the number of sources spanned. Every experiment
repeats over the same 10 random seeds and reports the mean and the standard deviation across them.
A seed fixes the 400 candidate assignments, and hence the selected split, as well as the
initialization of the model and the choice of negatives, so the 10 seeds yield 10 different
splits. Results therefore measure generalization to unseen sources rather than memorization of
seen ones. Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset} summarizes the cases.
