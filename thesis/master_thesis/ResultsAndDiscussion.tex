\section{Overall Comparison}
\label{sec:overall}

Table~\ref{tab:overall} and Fig.~\ref{fig:comparison} compare the three methods of Section~5.2.
% source: experiments/strat_A.json / strat_B.json results.*.Recall@K_correct
The proposed method raises Recall@K from 0.521 to 0.743 in Setting~A and from 0.466 to 0.689 in
Setting~B, a gain of $+0.222$ and $+0.223$ over the classical information-retrieval baseline. It
wins on all 10 seeds in both settings (Wilcoxon signed-rank, $p = 0.00195$; Cohen's $d_z = 11.9$
and 12.9), and the secondary metrics agree: MAP rises from 0.581 to 0.863 in Setting~A and from
0.512 to 0.832 in Setting~B, and Recall@20 reaches 0.909 and 0.838.
% source: experiments/significance_stats.json（p_wilcoxon 0.00195, cohen_dz 11.893 / 12.903）
% source: experiments/strat_A.json / strat_B.json results.*.MAP / Recall@20
The leftmost group of Fig.~\ref{fig:comparison} adds the full collection with the augmented cases
for reference; Section~5.1 explains why that setting flatters the baseline and why Settings A
and~B carry the evaluation.

LLM direct generation stays far below both learned rerankers. With equivalence-based scoring it
reaches Recall@K of 0.254 in Setting~A ($n = 48$), 0.226 in Setting~B ($n = 50$), and 0.300 on the
full collection ($n = 49$), while its rank-free coverage --- the fraction of ground-truth
equations for which \emph{some} generated equation is judged equivalent, ignoring rank --- reaches
0.419, 0.343, and 0.567.
% source: llm_set_A_equiv_results.json（0.2545/0.4188, n=48）, llm_set_B_equiv_results.json
%         （0.2258/0.3427, n=50）, llm_set_full_equiv_results.json（0.3003/0.5667, n=49）
The distance between the two numbers separates two abilities: the model generates a fair share of
the required equations but does not assemble them into the right set of size $K$. Part of the
remaining gap is structural. Many ground-truth equations are empirical correlations whose
coefficients were fitted in one source document, and a generator that has not read that document
cannot reproduce them verbatim; retrieval returns them with their provenance. Recall also that the
equivalence judge is the generator itself (Section~5.2), which favors the language model. Neither
classical retrieval nor direct generation solves the task alone --- the baseline ranks the right
equations by query--equation relevance only, and the language model composes plausible equations
without provenance --- and the rest of this chapter explains where and why the proposed combination
works.

\section{Performance by Case Characteristics}
\label{sec:bycharacteristics}

Fig.~\ref{fig:characteristic} stratifies the Setting-A results by three case characteristics, and
the first two panels show the same pattern: the harder the case, the larger the advantage.
% source: experiments/strat_A.json per_case（バケットは fig_characteristic と同一。
%         式数: base 0.849→0.281 / 10S 0.878→0.543, 差 +0.029 → +0.21〜+0.29;
%         入力変数: 差 +0.048(≤5) → +0.283(≥21); 出典数: +0.219/+0.225/+0.259）
By the number of ground-truth equations, the baseline falls from 0.849 on single-equation cases to
0.281 on ten-equation cases, while the proposed method falls only from 0.878 to 0.543; the gap
grows from $+0.03$ at one equation to between $+0.21$ and $+0.29$ at two equations and beyond. By
the number of input variables, the gap widens from $+0.05$ (five or fewer) to $+0.28$ (21 or
more). Both slices say the same thing: retrieving a single equation was nearly solved already, and
the advantage concentrates where the answer is a set and selection is combinatorial.

The number of source documents shows no such trend: the gap stays between $+0.22$ and $+0.26$
whether the ground truth spans one, two, or three or more documents. In our data the source count
is confounded with the equation count --- the synthesized cases have the largest answers but stay
within one document in 95.9\% of cases, while the cross-document cases span two documents but need
only about two equations (Table~\ref{tab:families}) --- so this slice mixes the case families
rather than isolating a third difficulty axis, and we do not interpret it causally.

\section{Mechanism: Which Features Cause the Improvement}
\label{sec:mechanism}

The gain of $+0.222$ in Setting~A decomposes into three steps, which we measure by growing the
feature set of the reranker and retraining at each step.
% source: experiments/xd/（10 seeds; reranker-2 0.6010, reranker-2+Spec 0.6908（+0.0898,
%         p_wilcoxon=0.00195）, reranker-2+InCov 0.6203, reranker-2+OutCov 0.6071,
%         reranker-2+Dom 0.6034, reranker-2+Svd 0.5989, reranker-7 0.6934, reranker-10S 0.7429）
First, learning alone: a reranker that sees only the two Stage-1 features but learns their
weighting (reranker-2) moves Recall@K from 0.521 to 0.601 ($+0.080$). Second, per-equation
features: growing the input to the seven features of Table~\ref{tab:features} adds another
$+0.092$ (0.601 to 0.693), and one feature carries almost all of it --- adding specificity alone
reaches 0.691 ($+0.090$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.00195$), while adding the domain match or the latent
similarity alone changes the score by $+0.002$ and $-0.002$ (Section~\ref{sec:nullresult}).
Third, the set-aware features: the step from reranker-7 to reranker-10S adds the remaining
$+0.050$ (Wilcoxon $p = 0.00195$, $d_z = 4.7$).
% source: experiments/feature_x_difficulty_stats.json overall_tests.reranker-10S
The set-aware contribution is therefore real but modest, and most of the improvement comes from
the reranker learning one lesson: an equation whose variables are specific to the requested
model probably belongs to it.

Which of the three set-aware features carries the $+0.050$? Adding each alone to reranker-7
answers directly: complementarity adds $+0.052$ and coherence $+0.050$ (each Wilcoxon
$p = 0.00195$), while domain agreement adds $-0.0001$ ($p = 0.98$).
% source: experiments/feature_x_difficulty_stats.json overall_tests（+Comp 0.0518 / +Coh 0.0497 /
%         +Dom -0.0001）
The two structural features are individually sufficient and largely redundant with each other ---
all three together add no more than one of them alone --- and the topical one contributes nothing,
a null result that Section~\ref{sec:nullresult} traces to its cause.

Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism} shows where the set-aware lift lives. Across the number of ground-truth
equations it has a threshold-and-hump shape: nothing on single-equation cases ($+0.002$, where no
reference set can add information), a peak at three equations ($+0.102$), and a settled $+0.02$
beyond. Across the number of input variables the lift climbs to $+0.078$ at 11--15 variables and
then drops to $+0.035$ at 16 or more.
% source: experiments/feature_x_difficulty_stats.json lift_by_n_correct / lift_by_n_input
%         （reranker-10S; X=1 +0.0022, X=3 +0.1018; 11-15 +0.0782, 16+ +0.0348）
The very hardest cases thus profit only half as much as the moderately hard ones. We attribute
this saturation to the static reference set: during static scoring the set-aware features are
computed once against the Stage-1 top five (Section~\ref{sec:stage2}), and in a long answer that fixed
reference resembles the true partial answer less and less as selection proceeds. The attribution
is testable --- a method that rebuilds the reference set during selection should recover the loss
at the hard end --- and Section~\ref{sec:greedyresults} runs exactly that intervention.

Ablation retrains the model for every feature set, so it measures \emph{necessity}: whether the
performance is recoverable without a feature. Permutation feature importance (PFI) asks the
complementary question --- how much the single trained model \emph{relies} on each feature --- by
permuting one feature's values across the candidates of each case and measuring the drop in
Recall@K, with no retraining.
% source: experiments/pfi_results.json results.case（10 seeds × 20 permutations; one-sample
%         t-test over seeds; GROUP_var +0.678 p<1e-4, GROUP_topic +0.0064 p=0.10,
%         specificity +0.617; global 置換 GROUP_var +0.675）
Fig.~\ref{fig:pfi} shows the result for the best model, reranker-10S. Permuting the six
variable-overlap features together collapses Recall@K from 0.743 to 0.065 (importance $+0.678$,
$p < 10^{-4}$); permuting the four topic features together changes it by $+0.006$ ($p = 0.10$).
The trained model relies most heavily on specificity ($+0.617$), every topic feature is
individually indistinguishable from zero, and permuting across the whole test set instead of
within each case gives the same group importance ($+0.675$ against $+0.678$), so the finding does
not depend on the permutation scope.

The two analyses disagree in one instructive place, and the disagreement is informative rather
than troubling. Specificity's reliance under PFI ($+0.617$) far exceeds its ablation gain over
reranker-2 ($+0.090$), because the two numbers answer different questions: ablation retrains, and
a retrained model recovers the variable-overlap signal from the remaining features of the group;
PFI freezes the weights, and the frozen model cannot re-route around a corrupted input. Read
together, they say that the variable-overlap \emph{signal} is necessary while the particular
feature carrying it is substitutable within the group. Where it matters, the two methods agree:
variable overlap carries the improvement, and topic carries none.

\section{Intervention: Learned Greedy Selection}
\label{sec:greedyresults}

Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} predicted that rebuilding the reference set during selection should
recover the saturation on the hardest cases. We test the prediction with the three selection
strategies of Section~\ref{sec:seq}, in Setting~B because its answers are the largest. These experiments
widen the Stage-1 window to $k = 200$, which lifts the candidate-coverage bound from 92.0\% to
97.8\% (Section~\ref{sec:stage1}); their static values therefore exceed the $k = 50$ values of
Table~\ref{tab:overall} and are comparable only within this section.
% top-k: run_greedy_3way.sh / run_greedy_3way_A.sh / run_dof_stop.sh いずれも TOPK=200
% source: experiments/greedy_3way_stats.json overall / tests（static 0.7237 < infer 0.7556 <
%         train 0.7649; infer_vs_static +0.0319 p_w=0.00195 dz=3.28;
%         train_vs_infer +0.0093 p_w=0.0371 dz=0.78）
The ordering comes out as predicted (Fig.~\ref{fig:greedy}): static 0.724 $<$ greedy at inference
0.756 $<$ learned greedy 0.765 over the same 10 seeds. Greedy selection at inference alone
recovers $+0.032$ (Wilcoxon $p = 0.00195$, $d_z = 3.3$), and training on the sequential contexts
adds a further $+0.009$ ($p = 0.037$, $d_z = 0.78$): a small increment with a clear reading ---
training must ask the same question as inference, namely which equation closes the system next,
given the ones already chosen.

The gain concentrates exactly where Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} located the saturation. On cases
with eight or more ground-truth equations, learned greedy beats static selection by $+0.056$
($p = 0.00195$, $d_z = 3.7$) and beats inference-only greedy by $+0.013$ ($p = 0.020$), its
largest margins of any stratum.
% source: greedy_3way_stats.json tests_hard_X8plus（train_vs_static +0.056; train_vs_infer
%         +0.0132 p_w=0.0195）
Setting~A repeats the pattern at smaller scale: static 0.757 $<$ inference greedy 0.787 $<$
learned greedy 0.797, with both greedy variants winning on all 10 seeds ($p = 0.00195$), while
the learned-versus-inference increment ($+0.010$) points the same way without reaching
significance ($p = 0.13$).
% source: experiments/greedy_3way_A_stats.json（0.7568/0.7865/0.7968; train_vs_infer p_w=0.131）
Setting~A dilutes the effect with the many small cases that sequential selection cannot help,
which is what the lift shape of Fig.~\ref{fig:mechanism} predicts.

The sequence matters more than the sizes. The mechanism analysis predicted a specific failure ---
the static reference set misrepresents long answers --- the intervention changed only that part of
the method, and the recovery appeared in the predicted stratum. This predict--intervene--recover
arc is what separates ``the set-aware features correlate with the improvement'' from ``the
set-aware features cause it.''

\section{Self-terminating Retrieval}
\label{sec:dofstopresults}

Every experiment so far told the method how many equations to return. DoF-stop removes that help:
it runs the same learned greedy selector and stops at the first prefix that covers every output
variable with no remaining degree of freedom (Section~\ref{sec:dofstop}). We compare it against
the oracle that cuts the same ranking at the true answer size.
% source: experiments/dof_stop_stats.json（learned greedy + stop-dof, top_k=200, 10 seeds）
Withholding the answer size costs almost nothing (Fig.~\ref{fig:dof}). The exact-match rate of
DoF-stop equals the oracle's not only on average but case by case --- 0.526 in Setting~A and
0.368 in Setting~B, with zero disagreements among the 7,026 per-case records --- because a
completely correct selection is itself a closed system, so the closure test fires at exactly the
point where the oracle would have cut.
% source: experiments/xd_dof/ per_case 全レコードで set_exact_dof == set_exact_oracleK（不一致 0）
The set-$F_1$ cost is $-0.008$ in Setting~A and $-0.023$ in Setting~B (paired $t$-test,
$p = 0.006$ and $p = 0.0001$): measurable and small.
% source: dof_stop_stats.json dof_vs_oracle_f1

On the property a model builder actually needs, DoF-stop beats the oracle instead of
approximating it: 93.6\% of its returned sets are closed in Setting~A against the oracle's
86.5\%, and 88.3\% against 78.1\% in Setting~B.
% source: dof_stop_stats.json closed_dof / closed_oracleK
The oracle returns exactly $K$ equations whether or not they form a solvable system; DoF-stop
keeps selecting until the system closes, so every set it terminates on is solvable by
construction, and the unclosed remainder (11.7\% in Setting~B) consists of the cases where no
prefix closed within the selection cap. Its size errors are one-sided in practice: the median
size error is zero, every erring record in Setting~B overshoots (545 of 2,432), and all but one
of the 4,594 records in Setting~A do. When DoF-stop misses the size, it has kept selecting past
the answer rather than stopped short of it (mean size error $+1.7$ equations in Setting~B).
% source: dof_stop_stats.json（K_median_abs_err 0.0; K_bias 1.7212 = K_MAE（B）, 0.9293 vs
%         0.9297（A）; per_case 実測: B は k_pred_dof < n_correct が 0/2,432、A は 1/4,594）

One property of the dataset keeps this claim honest. In our cases the answer size nearly equals
the number of output variables (Section~4.2), so predicting the size itself would be easy, and we
claim no difficulty there. What DoF-stop demonstrates is self-termination with a closure
guarantee: the retriever decides by itself when the model is complete, and what it returns can be
handed to a solver.

\begin{figure}[h]\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{figures/fig_dof_stop.pdf}
\caption{Self-terminating retrieval, both selectors running the same learned greedy model with
$k = 200$: (a) set $F_1$ of the oracle that knows the answer size and of DoF-stop (boxes over 10
seeds; the annotated closure rates show the fraction of returned sets that are closed);
(b) set quality by number of ground-truth equations in Setting B (mean $\pm$ SEM over seeds).}
\label{fig:dof}
\end{figure}

\section{The Null Result for Topic Similarity}
\label{sec:nullresult}

Four of the ten features read topical closeness: the text similarity $f_1$, the latent similarity
$f_3$, the domain match $f_7$, and the set-level domain agreement $g_{\mathrm{Dom}}$
(Table~\ref{tab:features}). None of them improves reranking in any form we tried. The result
deserves this section because the features rest on a reasonable expectation --- the equations of
one model should share a topic --- and because finding where the expectation breaks tells us what
signal the task actually rewards.

The failure spans every encoding of topic. Adding the domain match to reranker-2 moves Recall@K
by $+0.002$ (paired $t$-test $p = 0.063$, Wilcoxon $p = 0.123$); adding the latent similarity
instead moves it by $-0.002$ ($p = 0.48$); adding the set-level domain agreement to reranker-7
moves it by $-0.0001$ ($p = 0.98$; Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}); and in the trained ten-feature
model, permuting all four topic features together leaves Recall@K essentially unchanged
($+0.006$, $p = 0.10$), whereas permuting the variable-overlap group collapses it.
% source: experiments/xd/（reranker-2 0.6010 → +Dom 0.6034 p_t=0.063 / +Svd 0.5989 p_t=0.48）,
%         feature_x_difficulty_stats.json overall_tests.reranker-7+Dom,
%         pfi_results.json results.case GROUP_topic

An obvious suspect is the label vocabulary. The domain labels are free text written by the
extraction model, and Section~4.1 measured the consequence: 1,998 distinct labels over the 11,146
equations, 27.9\% occurring exactly once, 6.36 labels within an average source document, and
near-duplicates such as ``Transport Phenomena'' and ``Transport phenomena'' counted as distinct.
Both domain features therefore fire sparsely. Merging the labels tests this suspicion directly:
normalized to 1,958 groups, or clustered into 100 or 40 topical groups --- the scale of the
collection taxonomy --- the domain match adds at most $+0.0006$ (paired $t$-test, all $p > 0.6$),
and even the $+0.002$ of the raw labels disappears.
% source: experiments/domain_merge_stats.json（L1 +0.0006 p_t=0.68; K100 -0.0001 p_t=0.84;
%         K40 +0.0004 p_t=0.63; run_domain_merge_ablation.py）
A finer taxonomy would deepen the sparsity, and a coarser one does not help either, so sparsity is
not the obstacle.

The aggregate zero of the domain agreement is, moreover, a balance rather than an absence. We
split the evaluation cases by whether the feature happens to separate toward the answer within
that case's candidates, counting each case once (its permutation drop averaged over the seeds in
which it appears, which avoids treating the same case as ten observations). Where the feature
separates toward the answer (1,169 cases), permuting it costs 0.012 of Recall@K; where it
separates away (414 cases), permuting it \emph{gains} 0.034; the two groups differ (Mann--Whitney
$p = 1.3 \times 10^{-8}$), and their weighted sum reproduces the observed zero ($-0.0002$).
% source: experiments/pfi_profile_stats.json features.gDom.cancellation_all_cases
%         （+0.0119 (n=1,169) / -0.0342 (n=414); 加重和 (1169*0.0119-414*0.0342)/1583 = -0.0002;
%         残る 9 ケースは sep=0。analyze_pfi_profile.py collapse_to_cases でケース集約済み）
The trained model does listen to the label case by case; the label misleads it often enough to
cancel the cases where it helps. Any future use of this feature must first make the label point
the right way more often --- and the merging experiment shows that vocabulary cleanup alone does
not.

The latent similarity fails for a different and more instructive reason, which a diagnosis
requiring no trained model locates (Fig.~\ref{fig:topicdiag}). A natural hypothesis says the
TF-IDF--SVD projection crowds the query vectors together until cases become indistinguishable.
Panel~(a) rejects it: the cosine similarity between the query vectors of two cases has median
0.218 in the SVD space and 0.070 in the raw TF-IDF space, and it exceeds 0.5 for only 5.4\% of
pairs.
% source: experiments/topic_feature_diagnosis.json A_case_vector_similarity（SVD median 0.2178,
%         raw 0.0702, share>0.5 5.4%; 200,000 sampled pairs）
Decisively, the latent similarity alone separates the ground-truth equations from the rest of the
11,146-equation database with an area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve (AUC) of
0.950 (case mean; standard error 0.003) --- impossible if the vectors had collapsed. The signal
exists; it is spent in the wrong place. Panel~(b) compares each feature's AUC against the whole
database with its AUC inside the top-50 candidates: the latent similarity falls from 0.950 to
0.594 and the text similarity from 0.913 to 0.437, while specificity holds 0.965 and the I/O
Jaccard 0.947.
% source: topic_feature_diagnosis.json D_full_db_auc（svd 0.950±0.003, text 0.913±0.003,
%         io_jaccard 0.999）/ C_within_candidate_auc（svd 0.594±0.006, text 0.437±0.007,
%         specificity 0.965±0.001, io_jaccard 0.947±0.002）
Stage~1 has already done the coarse screening that topical similarity is good at, so the
surviving candidates agree on topic and differ on variables; what remains to be discriminated,
topic does not discriminate.

Candidate selection even reverses the surface text similarity. Stage~1 admits an equation for a
weighted sum of text similarity and variable overlap (Eq.~\eqref{eq:stage1}), so inside the
admitted set the two ingredients correlate at $-0.404$: an admitted equation with high text
similarity tends to be one that needed the text term to get in, that is, one whose variables
match poorly.
% source: experiments/case_outcomes_seed42.json selection_effect_corr（corr(text_sim,io_jaccard)
%         = -0.404, corr(text_sim,specificity) = -0.392; seed 42, 423 cases）
Because the answers are decided on the variable side, the text similarity points slightly away
from them within the candidates (AUC 0.437). A two-stage design therefore spends topical
similarity exactly once, in Stage~1, and gains nothing by spending it twice.

\section{Error Analysis: Where the Remaining Errors Come From}
\label{sec:erroranalysis}

The proposed method still misses about a quarter of the ground-truth equations in Setting~A, and
this section locates the misses. The candidate window bounds the reranker from above: the top
$k = 50$ candidates contain 92.0\% of the ground-truth equations, and all of them for only 77.2\%
of the cases; at $k = 200$ the two numbers rise to 97.8\% and 90.3\% (Section~\ref{sec:stage1}).
Decomposing the misses of one split accordingly (seed 42; 435 missed equations across 211
partially or fully failed cases), 61.8\% never entered the candidate set, and 38.2\% entered it
and ranked below the cutoff. At $k = 50$, the first stage, not the reranker, causes the larger
share of the residual error.
% source: experiments/case_outcomes_seed42.json failure_decomposition（435 = 269 + 166）
Widening the window moves this balance without removing the errors: at $k = 200$ the same split
misses 373 equations, the Stage-1 share falls to 28.4\%, and ranking errors dominate (71.6\%).
The first stage therefore bounds the $k = 50$ configuration of Table~\ref{tab:overall}, while the
sequential experiments of Sections \ref{sec:greedyresults} and \ref{sec:dofstopresults}, which
already run at $k = 200$, are bounded by the reranker.
% source: experiments/case_outcomes_seed42_k200.json failure_decomposition（373 = 106 + 267,
%         stage1_share 0.284; Recall@K 0.791→0.807）

The Stage-1 blend itself sets part of the bound. Ranking the database by variable overlap alone
covers 96.1\% of the ground-truth equations within 50 candidates, against 92.0\% for the blend of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:stage1}: at the margin, the text term costs coverage for the same reason it fails
inside the window (Section~\ref{sec:nullresult}).
% source: topic_feature_diagnosis.json E_stage1_coverage（io_jaccard_only 0.961±0.002 vs
%         blend 0.920±0.004）
This measurement predicts that a variable-weighted first stage would raise the ceiling, and we
designate that redesign as the next intervention experiment.

Failed cases differ from solved cases in size, not in kind. They average 5.34 ground-truth
equations against 2.13 and 17.8 input variables against 10.8 (Mann--Whitney, both
$p < 10^{-14}$), and their candidate coverage is 0.83 against 1.00.
% source: case_outcomes_seed42.json solved_vs_unsolved_attrs（212 solved / 211 unsolved）
For the misses that do survive into the candidate set, no present feature would repair the
ranking: pairing each missed equation with each wrong equation ranked above it, the missed one
has the higher latent similarity in 0.516 of the pairs (chance level), the higher domain
agreement in 0.494, and the higher variable overlap in only 0.375 --- the wrong equation usually
looks at least as good on every feature the model has.
% source: case_outcomes_seed42.json topic_rescue_fraction（131 cases with reranker misses）

Two concrete error types make up that residue. First, standard physical equations recur across
documents, so the model picks a mathematically identical equation from the wrong document: in one
batch-fermentation case the answer $\mathrm{d}X/\mathrm{d}t = \mu X$ lost to the same growth
equation printed in another document, which the topic features scored \emph{higher}. Strengthening
topical similarity would make this error worse. Second, the features read only variable sets, so
two equations that determine different variables from the same variables are indistinguishable:
the paired equations for $\mathrm{d}C_A'/\mathrm{d}t$ and $\mathrm{d}T'/\mathrm{d}t$ of one
linearized reactor tie on every feature, and the wrong one can rank first. The natural repair is a
feature that encodes which variable an equation determines; neither error type yields to more
topical information.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Six limitations qualify these results. First, the descriptions of the synthesized cases are
written by a language model, so their style may differ from human modeling requests; the
equations, inputs, and outputs of every case follow from the rule-based construction
(Section~4.3). Second, the number of source documents is confounded with the number of equations,
so Section~\ref{sec:bycharacteristics} draws no causal conclusion from it. Third, the stratified
split is one scheme selected from 400 candidates; the 10 seeds average over the randomness within
the scheme, not over schemes. Fourth, the evaluation is database-internal --- retrieval cannot
find an equation absent from the database --- and the language-model comparison rests on about 50
cases per setting with generator and judge being the same model. Fifth, the variable-overlap
group alone is highly predictive in this benchmark: permuting it costs 0.68 of Recall@K
(Section~\ref{sec:mechanism}), so part of the advantage reflects how the cases were constructed
from variable-sharing equations, and transfer to queries whose variable notation differs from the
database remains untested. Sixth, learned greedy trains with teacher forcing: during training the
reference set is the true partial answer, while at inference it contains the model's own
mistakes; scheduled sampling is the standard remedy, and we leave it to future work.
