This thesis formulated one step of automated physical model building as a retrieval problem and
addressed it with a method that evaluates candidate equations as a set. Given a description of the
desired model and its input and output variables, the method presents, from a database of 11,146
equations drawn from 361 sources, the 20 equations most likely to serve in the model, and the
engineer assembles and checks the model from them. Because an equation that contains a variable
other than the inputs and outputs is useful only together with an equation that determines that
variable, the method scores each candidate not only by its relevance to the query but also by its
relation to other equations: a first stage narrows the database to the candidates by text
similarity and variable overlap, and a second stage reranks the candidates with a multilayer
perceptron that reads three set-aware features --- complementarity, coherence, and domain agreement
--- beside seven query--equation features. A greedy variant selects one equation at a time and
learns, with teacher forcing, which equation to select next.

The thesis makes three contributions. First, it provides a benchmark on which a failure can be
attributed to the method: all 1,731 evaluation ground truths meet the first three requirements of
Kato and Kano and let each unknown be assigned to an equation of its own, 1,000 of them are
synthesized by a program that grows a ground truth from a differential equation, and stratified
source-disjoint splits with 10 seeds measure generalization to unseen sources, with validation
cases choosing the hyperparameters. Second, with a first stage that admits every ground-truth equation
($N_1 = 2{,}000$), the method raises Recall@20 over classical retrieval, whose weights the
validation cases choose, from 0.906 to 0.981 in the mixed setting and from 0.825 to 0.966 in the
synthesized-only setting, and MAP from 0.690 to 0.860 and from 0.626 to 0.832; it wins on every
metric in all 10 splits and also beats the variants of the baseline with BM25 and E5. The lead grows
with the numbers of ground-truth equations and input variables. Third, the thesis explains the
gain. Ablation and permutation importance agree that the features reading variable overlap ---
specificity above all --- decide most of the accuracy, and that the set-aware features mainly raise
the ground-truth equations within the top 20 (MAP $+0.073$). The topic features still separate the
ground-truth equations within the candidates, yet add little next to the variable-overlap features.
Learned greedy selection raises MAP over static reranking by $+0.032$ on the cases with eight or
more ground-truth equations. Every ground-truth equation that misses the top 20 is missed by the second stage, and
the present features cannot repair these misses.

Several directions follow from the limitations of Section~\ref{sec:limitations}. First, the
comparison with large language models prepared in Section~\ref{sec:compared} should be run. Second,
the data need cleaning: the database should be freed of duplicate sources and of identical equations
taken from different documents, and the cases should be reviewed for symbols that denote different
quantities in different equations. Third,
the ranking errors that remain lie outside every current feature: an equation that determines the
wrong variable calls for a feature that encodes which variable an equation determines, and the
comparison between equations, which now reads only their variable sets, could borrow the tree-based
and embedding representations of formula search (Section~\ref{sec:rw-mathir}). Learned greedy
selection, which still trains with teacher forcing, could adopt scheduled sampling. Fourth, the
evaluation is internal to the database and the variable notation of the queries matches the
database; validation on real modeling tasks, including requests whose equations are absent from the
database or written in a different notation, will show how far the results carry. Finally, the
method should be integrated into the AutoPMoB pipeline, downstream of equation extraction and
variable-equivalence judgment and upstream of the combination method of Kato and
Kano\cite{CombineEq}, so that the presented equations can be assembled into a model, solved, and
compared with plant data --- the test that automated model building must finally pass.
